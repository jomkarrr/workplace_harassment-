# Workplace Harassment — Topic Deep-Dive

Detailed notes behind the landing page. Useful for presentations, viva and reports.

## 1. Definition

Workplace harassment is any **unwelcome behaviour, comment or action** that creates a
hostile, intimidating or offensive working environment — whether it happens once or
repeatedly. The ILO's Violence and Harassment Convention, 2019 (No. 190) describes it
as behaviour that aims at, results in, or is likely to result in **physical,
psychological, sexual or economic harm**.

A **harassment-free workplace** is one where every employee can work with dignity and
safety, regardless of gender, caste, religion, age, disability or position.

## 2. Types of Harassment

| # | Type | Examples |
|---|------|----------|
| 1 | Sexual | Unwelcome advances, demand for sexual favours, sexually coloured remarks, showing pornography, unwanted touching |
| 2 | Verbal | Abusive language, slurs, insulting jokes, threats, shouting |
| 3 | Psychological / Bullying | Mobbing, isolation, damaging a person's reputation, impossible targets, withholding information |
| 4 | Physical | Pushing, hitting, unwanted contact, blocking a person's path |
| 5 | Discriminatory | Bias based on gender, caste, religion, disability, age or similar grounds |
| 6 | Cyber | Abusive messages, rumours or images over email, chat or social media — including in remote / work-from-home settings |

Two important forms:

- **Quid Pro Quo** — "Do this favour if you want the promotion." A misuse of power.
- **Hostile Work Environment** — the day-to-day atmosphere itself becomes so toxic that working becomes difficult.

## 3. Impact

- **On the victim:** anxiety, depression, PTSD, sleep problems, loss of self-esteem, and physical effects such as blood-pressure and heart problems.
- **On witnesses and the team:** loss of trust and morale; other employees also feel unsafe.
- **On the organisation:** lower productivity, absenteeism, high attrition, legal costs and reputational damage.
- **On society:** healthcare costs and wasted talent.

**Key statistic:** In the ILO's global study, **more than 1 in 5 (22.8%) employed
persons** had experienced at least one form of violence and harassment at work during
their working life.

## 4. The Law in India — POSH Act, 2013

**Full name:** The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition
and Redressal) Act, 2013.
**Origin:** It builds on the Supreme Court's **Vishaka Guidelines (1997)** in
*Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan*.

**Three pillars:** Prevention · Prohibition · Redressal

### Key provisions

- Applies to **all workplaces** in India, including remote work.
- Every workplace with **10 or more employees** must constitute an **Internal
  Complaints Committee (ICC)**.
- **ICC composition:** a senior woman employee as Presiding Officer, at least two
  internal members, one external member (NGO / subject expert), and **at least 50%
  women members**.
- **Section 2(n)** covers: physical contact and advances; a demand or request for
  sexual favours; sexually coloured remarks; showing pornography; and any other
  unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature.
- **Timelines:** a complaint must be filed within **3 months** of the incident
  (extendable by 3 more months); the ICC must complete its inquiry within
  **90 days**; the employer must act on the report within 60 days.
- Workplaces with fewer than 10 employees, or complaints against the employer
  himself, go to the **Local Complaints Committee (LCC)** at district level.
- **Employer duties (Section 19):** provide a safe working environment, display the
  POSH policy and ICC details, run regular awareness programmes, treat sexual
  harassment as misconduct under service rules, and file an annual report with the
  District Officer.
- **Penalties:** a fine of up to **₹50,000** for non-compliance; for a repeat
  offence the fine is doubled and the business licence / registration can be
  cancelled.
- **Confidentiality is mandatory**, and **retaliation against a complainant is
  prohibited**.

> Note: The POSH Act specifically protects women. Many organisations adopt
> gender-neutral internal policies so that every employee is covered.

## 5. Building a Harassment-Free Workplace — Prevention Framework

1. **Clear zero-tolerance policy** — what is acceptable and what is not, in writing.
2. **ICC and a safe reporting channel** — confidential, without fear.
3. **Regular training** — at least annually for all employees, separately for managers.
4. **Bystander intervention — the 5 D's:**
   - **Direct** — say clearly that the behaviour is not okay
   - **Distract** — change the topic / interrupt the situation
   - **Delegate** — inform HR / the ICC
   - **Document** — keep a record of what happened
   - **Delay** — support the victim afterwards
5. **Leadership and culture** — respect must show in appraisals and promotions, not only on posters.

## 6. If It Happens to You

1. If it is safe, say clearly: *"This is not okay. Stop."*
2. **Document everything** — date, time, place, what happened, witnesses; save screenshots and emails.
3. File a **written complaint** with HR / the ICC within 3 months.
4. Report any retaliation separately — that is also an offence.
5. **Help:** 112 (Emergency) · 1091 / 181 (Women Helpline) · National Commission for Women · SHe-Box portal (shebox.wcd.gov.in)

## 7. Myths vs Facts

| Myth | Fact |
|------|------|
| "It was just a joke; the intent was not wrong." | The law and good policy judge the **impact**, not the intent. |
| "A senior cannot commit harassment." | A power difference makes harassment **more** serious, not less. |
| "Complaining will end my career." | The law protects complainants — confidentiality is mandatory and retaliation is prohibited. |
| "It only counts if it is physical." | Verbal, psychological, discriminatory and cyber conduct also count. |
